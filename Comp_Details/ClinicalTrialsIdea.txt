
Here is exactly how TrialMatch will function from the moment a doctor opens the application to the moment the patient leaves the clinic. We will design this as a 5-step automated pipeline that completely removes the administrative friction of clinical trials.
1. The Clinical Ingestion (The Input)
The doctor has a patient sitting in the exam room who has just failed their last round of standard chemotherapy.
The Action: The doctor opens the TrialMatch dashboard and drags-and-drops the patient’s unstructured clinical notes, recent lab results, and genetic biomarker panel (e.g., a PDF) into the interface.
The AI Layer: Behind the scenes, your LangChain/LLM agent instantly parses this unstructured text. It extracts the critical variables required for trial matching: exact disease stage, specific genetic mutations (e.g., KRAS G12C), previous failed drugs, age, and organ function baselines.
2. The Smart Screener (The Match)
Clinical trial inclusion/exclusion criteria are notoriously dense, often spanning pages of complex medical requirements.
The Action: Your backend pings the ClinicalTrials.gov v2 API alongside Impiricus’s internal database of sponsored pharma trials.
The AI Layer: The agent acts as a semantic cross-referencer. It doesn't just do a keyword search; it uses a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) pipeline to compare the patient's extracted clinical variables against the strict legal inclusion/exclusion rules of active trials, filtering out anything where the patient wouldn't qualify.
3. The HCP Interface (The Output)
The doctor is presented with a clean, highly visual dashboard showing the top 3 best-matching clinical trials.
The UI: For each trial, the dashboard provides a "Match Confidence Score" and visually highlights exactly why the patient qualifies (e.g., "Patient has required KRAS mutation AND meets the required kidney function baseline").
The Commercial Fit: The dashboard subtly prioritizes trials sponsored by Impiricus's pharmaceutical clients, ensuring the sponsor gets massive B2B value by directing high-intent doctors to their specific trials.
4. The Impiricus Handoff (The B2B Connection)
Normally, if a doctor finds a trial, they have to blindly call a massive research hospital and leave a voicemail trying to refer their patient.
The Action: In TrialMatch, the doctor simply clicks a button that says "Initiate Trial Referral."
The AI Layer: Because this is built on Impiricus, the platform automatically routes the doctor's referral request directly to the exact Principal Investigator or pharma Trial Coordinator responsible for that specific trial. It replaces a week of phone tag with a single digital ping.
5. The "Magic Moment" (The Patient Translation)
This is the feature that will win you the hackathon. The biggest barrier to patient enrollment is the terrifying, 50-page legal consent document full of medical jargon.
The Action: When the doctor selects a trial, they click "Generate Patient Brief."
The AI Layer: The LLM ingests the massive, complex trial protocol and instantly generates a 2-page, 8th-grade reading-level summary. It explains the goal of the trial, the experimental drug, the potential side effects, and the schedule—all in plain, compassionate English (or translated into the patient's native language).
The Result: The doctor prints this out and hands it to the patient right there in the exam room, turning a terrifying legal document into an accessible conversation.




Part 1: The Reality of TrialMatch
Who does it target? It targets Community Oncologists (local cancer doctors) and specialists dealing with rare or terminal diseases. Over 80% of cancer patients are treated in these local clinics, not massive academic research hospitals like Johns Hopkins. How big of an issue is it? It is a massive failure of the medical system. Currently, only about 5% to 8% of adult cancer patients enroll in clinical trials. How common does it go to this level? Community doctors simply do not have the time to sift through ClinicalTrials.gov, read 50-page inclusion criteria, and explain the legal consent forms to patients while seeing 30 people a day. Because of this administrative friction, doctors often stick to standard chemotherapy even when a trial might be better. The Fix (Without pandering to Impiricus): Instead of only showing Impiricus trials, the tool is completely agnostic and shows all trials. The "Commercial Fit" is that pharma companies pay Impiricus to sponsor the workflow. Think of it like GoodRx: they show you all the drug prices, but pharmaceutical companies pay them for featured placement or to sponsor the "One-Click Handoff" feature. It is a highly emotional, patient-centric pitch that is easy for general judges to understand.
